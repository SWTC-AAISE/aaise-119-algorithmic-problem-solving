# STANDARD STUDENT AI USE POLICY

---

# Purpose

This policy defines a general standard for how students may and may not use AI-assisted tools in coursework.

It is designed to be reusable across courses and instructors.

Course-specific assignments, rubrics, and addendums may further clarify or restrict AI use for a particular class. When a course-specific rule exists, that rule takes precedence over the general standard.

---

# Core Principle

AI may assist student work.

AI may not replace student responsibility for understanding, testing, revising, and explaining that work.

If a student uses AI, the student remains responsible for:

* understanding what was produced
* checking whether it is correct
* revising or rejecting weak output
* explaining the final result
* disclosing AI use when required

---

# What Is Generally Allowed

When permitted by the course or assignment, AI may be used for:

* brainstorming possible approaches
* clarifying unfamiliar vocabulary, syntax, or concepts
* generating an initial draft after the student has defined the problem
* suggesting revisions, refactoring, or organizational improvements
* helping identify bugs after the student has attempted diagnosis
* comparing alternate approaches to a problem
* extending or improving an existing solution

Allowed use always remains subject to assignment-specific instructions.

---

# What Is Generally Not Allowed

The following uses of AI are not acceptable under this standard:

* submitting AI-generated work that the student cannot explain
* copying AI output without checking, testing, or revising it
* using AI to bypass work that is intended to be completed independently
* hiding AI use when disclosure is required
* presenting AI-generated output as if no human decisions were involved
* using AI in ways that violate assignment-specific or course-specific rules

If a student cannot explain the work clearly, the student does not yet own the solution.

---

# Manual-First Possibility

Some assignments, labs, quizzes, or checkpoints may be intentionally manual-first.

This means students may be expected to:

* solve the task without AI
* attempt a manual solution before asking for AI support
* diagnose a problem themselves before using AI for debugging help

Manual-first expectations are a legitimate part of course design and may vary by course.

---

# Bounded AI Use

Different courses may introduce AI at different times and with different limits.

In some courses, AI may be restricted early and expanded later.

In others, AI may be available sooner but still remain bounded by student understanding and disclosure expectations.

Students should always check:

* whether AI is allowed for the task
* what kind of AI use is allowed
* whether disclosure is required

---

# Disclosure and Accountability

When AI use must be disclosed, students may be asked to explain:

* what they were trying to do
* what they used AI for
* what AI produced
* what they accepted, changed, or rejected
* how they verified correctness or quality
* what decisions remained their own

Courses may require this disclosure informally, through assignment notes, or through a formal justification document.

---

# Student Responsibilities

If a student uses AI, the student is expected to:

* define the problem clearly before relying on AI
* inspect the output carefully
* test or evaluate the result appropriately
* revise unclear, incorrect, or low-quality output
* ask questions when AI output does not make sense
* be honest about how the work was produced

---

# Instructor Responsibilities

Instructors using this standard should:

* clarify when AI use is allowed, restricted, or not allowed
* identify manual-first activities when they are part of the course design
* evaluate student understanding rather than output alone
* create disclosure expectations that match the course level and assignment type
* help students learn responsible AI use rather than treating AI only as a compliance issue

---

# Practical Rule for Students

Before using AI, students should ask:

* Do I understand the task?
* Am I allowed to use AI here?
* Will I be able to explain and evaluate what it gives me?

After using AI, students should ask:

* Do I understand this result now?
* Did I verify that it works or meets the requirement?
* What did I change, reject, or decide myself?

---

# Final Standard

The main question is not:

* "Did AI help produce this work?"

The main question is:

* "Can the student understand, justify, and explain the final result?"

If the answer is yes, then AI has been used responsibly within this standard.
